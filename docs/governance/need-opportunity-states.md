# Need and Opportunity Governance

## 1. Why separate states matter

The Atlas must distinguish what someone has reported from what has been corroborated, independently verified, or actively solicited. These labels describe evidence and operational status; they are not interchangeable.

## 2. Need evidence states

| State | Meaning | Minimum handling |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate | Potential need inferred or discovered, not yet assessed | Internal review only |
| Reported | A source or person asserts that a need exists | Preserve source and attribution |
| Under review | Assessment is in progress | Do not imply verification |
| Corroborated | Multiple sufficiently independent sources support the claim | Record sources and independence rationale |
| Verified | Evidence meets a defined verification method | Record method, date, reviewer, scope |
| Disputed | Material contradictory evidence exists | Preserve both sides; explain dispute |
| Stale | Evidence may no longer describe current conditions | Recheck before reliance |
| Closed | Need no longer active or no longer tracked | Record closure reason and date |

Verification is always scoped. For example, evidence may verify that a program requested volunteer tutors in a particular period, but not prove that the need remains open today.

## 3. Opportunity operational states

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Draft | Incomplete working record |
| Submitted | Awaiting review |
| Under review | Moderation or confirmation underway |
| Published | Meets publication requirements |
| Paused | Temporarily unavailable or under reassessment |
| Filled | Capacity has been met |
| Completed | Defined activity has concluded |
| Expired | Validity period elapsed |
| Withdrawn | Responsible party removed the opportunity |
| Rejected | Does not meet publication criteria |

## 4. Publication gates

Before publication, check:
1. A clear action and intended result are described.
2. The referenced need is characterized with its evidence state.
3. A responsible organization/contact or safe application mechanism is identified where applicable.
4. Location and time constraints are clear enough for discovery.
5. Requirements, costs, qualifications, and accessibility constraints are disclosed where known.
6. The source and last-checked date are recorded.
7. Personal data and sensitive details are minimized.
8. The record does not imply endorsement or verification beyond the evidence.

## 5. Expiration and revalidation

Every active listing should have a review interval or expiry date. Revalidation should check the responsible party, availability, capacity, dates, eligibility, contact channel, and whether the underlying need persists. Lack of a response is not proof that a need is false; it may justify marking the opportunity stale or unconfirmed.

## 6. Disputes and corrections

- Preserve prior versions and conflicting evidence.
- Record who made each assertion and when.
- Do not silently overwrite a material correction.
- Distinguish a correction to a factual field from a change in policy or interpretation.
- Provide a review path for organizations and affected communities.
- Record the rationale and evidence for a decision.

## 7. Public-facing labels

Prefer precise labels such as:
- Reported by source
- Corroborated by multiple sources
- Independently reviewed
- Organization-confirmed
- Last checked on [date]
- Status not recently confirmed

Avoid unqualified badges such as “verified” unless the verification method and scope are accessible.
