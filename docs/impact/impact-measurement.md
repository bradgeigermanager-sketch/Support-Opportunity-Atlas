# Impact Measurement and Evaluation Model

## 1. Measurement chain

Distinguish:
- **Inputs:** time, money, equipment, knowledge, facilities.
- **Activities:** tutoring, building, advising, distributing, researching.
- **Outputs:** direct deliverables or services, such as sessions delivered or tools released.
- **Outcomes:** changes observed among participants or systems.
- **Impact:** broader and longer-term effects, often influenced by many actors and conditions.

An output is not automatically an outcome, and an outcome is not automatically attributable to one project.

## 2. Indicator definition

Each indicator should include:
- stable identifier and name;
- definition and intended interpretation;
- unit and population;
- numerator/denominator where applicable;
- baseline and target;
- measurement period and frequency;
- collection method and source;
- disaggregation rules and privacy limits;
- responsible owner;
- limitations and known bias.

## 3. Baselines and targets

A target is a planned value, not a result. A baseline describes the starting condition and must include its measurement date and method. If no baseline exists, state that clearly rather than substituting zero.

## 4. Attribution and contribution

Record whether a result is:
- directly delivered by the project;
- associated with the project;
- plausibly contributed to by the project;
- independently evaluated as attributable under a defined method.

Avoid claiming that all people reached experienced a measurable benefit. Report reach, participation, completion, and outcome measures separately.

## 5. Example

Illustrative tutoring program:
- Input: 120 volunteer hours.
- Activity: 40 tutoring sessions.
- Output: 20 learners attended at least one session.
- Outcome indicator: change in a defined numeracy assessment between baseline and follow-up.
- Impact hypothesis: improved numeracy may contribute to longer-term educational opportunity.

The example does not assert that the outcome occurred. A real report would need baseline data, follow-up data, consent/ethics safeguards, and limitations.

## 6. Evaluation cycle

1. Define the problem and intended change.
2. Identify the target population and context.
3. Select measurable outputs and outcomes.
4. Record baseline, targets, and collection methods.
5. Collect data at defined intervals.
6. Analyze missingness, bias, and confounding factors.
7. Compare results with baseline and targets.
8. Document limitations and alternative explanations.
9. Publish appropriately aggregated results.
10. Use findings to improve the opportunity or close it.

## 7. Privacy and responsible reporting

Collect only what is necessary. Prefer aggregate reporting and suppress small cells where re-identification is possible. Do not expose personal case histories or sensitive demographic data in public impact dashboards.
